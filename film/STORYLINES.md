# Bulkhead — storylines

**Picked: A, "Damage control".** A stranger already knows the image: a ship that floods one compartment and survives because the doors shut. The product's name is that idea, so the picture is the pitch.

## A. Damage control (picked)
- **Device and form:** the object is the explanation, as a flight through one world. The data center is drawn as a ship's compartment plan: four halls, thousands of cells. The breach floods one compartment orange, and the camera travels the plan as defender agents shut its doors one by one. At each stop the real dashboard proves the step.
- **First 2 s:** the full hull plan fills the frame, 61,207 cells shimmering. One cell in Hall B flashes orange.
- **Beats:** fleet → breach floods toward host → old way (playbook no match, page unanswered) while flood rises → BULKHEAD door slams (drop) → Sentry (real signals) → Triage (real attack chain; blast radius lit on the plan) → Warden (doors shut: network, credentials, cordon) → evidence (guests evacuated to clean halls) → human approves (real ⌘↵) → last door slams, SEALED 9.4 s → Scribe report (real) → Nemotron on-prem, policy is the lock → SpacetimeDB log, every console in sync → door end card.
- **Look:** primer-grey painted steel, stencil black, safety orange as flood/breach, green only for "sealed"; Big Shoulders Stencil display.
- **Sound:** a low tense pulse while the water rises, a heavy steel door slam as the drop and on each sealed door, a full groove after the reveal, a final slam on the end card.

## B. Sleep through it
- **Device and form:** a moment in someone's day, as split screen to one. On the left the on-call engineer sleeps while the phone glows on the nightstand; on the right Bulkhead's agents work in the real UI. The panes collapse into one when the single approval needs her, and she wakes to a finished report.
- **Look and sound:** a dark bedroom with warm lamp light against the cold UI; quiet ambience that the groove takes over.

## C. Nine point four
- **Device and form:** a number lands, cut on the beat. A giant stopwatch runs the whole film, and each second of the 9.4 is one beat: one agent, one verb, one real UI punch-in. The film is exactly as long as the incident, stretched.
- **Look and sound:** a black-and-orange split-flap timer; a ticking metronome under a techno build.
